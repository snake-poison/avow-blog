A storm, a fire or a burst pipe sets a clock running on your insurance claim. What you do in the first two days decides much of what you can prove later.

## 1. Make it safe

Get people and pets out of danger first. If there is a gas smell, standing water near outlets or structural damage, leave and call the utility or emergency services before anything else.

## 2. Stop the damage from getting worse

Your policy almost certainly requires you to take reasonable steps to protect the property from further damage. That means temporary fixes:

- Shut off the water at the main for a leak.
- Tarp a damaged roof or board a broken window.
- Move undamaged belongings out of harm's way.

Keep the receipts for every one of these. Reasonable mitigation costs are usually covered.

## 3. Document before you clean

Photos and video taken before cleanup are the strongest evidence you will ever have.

- Walk every affected room with video, narrating what you see.
- Photograph damage up close and from across the room.
- Photograph serial and model numbers on damaged appliances.
- Do not throw damaged items away until they have been documented, and ideally seen by the adjuster.

## 4. Report the claim

Call your insurer or use their app, and write down the claim number, the date and who you spoke to. Ask for a copy of your full policy, including the declarations page and endorsements.

## 5. Start a claim file

Keep one folder, paper or digital, with every photo, receipt, letter and note of every call. A claim can run for months, and the file is what lets you, or a public adjuster working for you, answer every question the insurer asks.
